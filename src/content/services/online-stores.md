A storefront is the only kind of site where a defect has an immediate, countable price. A slow product page costs sessions. A checkout that fails under load costs orders you can name. An inventory feed that lags by an hour costs oversells and refunds.

Which means commerce work is mostly systems work. The storefront is the visible part; the integrations behind it are where the money is either collected or lost.

## Where this usually goes wrong

**Catalogue treated as a content problem.** Products modelled as pages rather than as structured data with variants, stock, pricing rules and availability. It works until you need a filter, a bundle, a regional price or a feed for a shopping channel.

**Checkout customised without a load path.** Custom checkouts convert better when they work. They also concentrate all your risk into the one flow you cannot afford to break, and most are never tested against the traffic shape of a peak day.

**Inventory synced optimistically.** One-way syncs on a timer, no reconciliation, no handling for the failure case. Oversells appear at exactly the moment volume is highest and support is busiest.

**Product pages that are slow where it counts.** Image-heavy templates that pass a homepage speed test and fail on the pages that actually convert.

**No structured data on products.** Missing price, availability and review markup means no rich results in a category of search where competitors have them — a visible gap on the result page itself.

**Subscriptions bolted on.** Recurring billing added late, with dunning, pauses, upgrades and failed payments handled ad hoc, quietly leaking revenue that nobody attributes to it.

## How we run it

We model the catalogue first — variants, stock, pricing, availability, and how those reach the storefront, the feeds and the fulfilment side. Nearly every commerce problem that surfaces later is a modelling decision made here.

Checkout is treated as the critical path it is: designed for the failure cases as much as the happy path, and exercised against peak-shaped traffic rather than average traffic.

Integrations are built with reconciliation, not just synchronisation — payments, inventory, fulfilment and any ERP or POS the business already runs, with a defined answer for what happens when one of them is unavailable.

Performance and structured data are built into the product and category templates, because these are the pages that earn the traffic and take the orders.

## The part most agencies cannot do

Commerce is where the split between growth and build costs the most. A shopping feed rejects products because the data model is wrong. A campaign needs a landing page that a template cannot express. A conversion problem sits in the checkout, which belongs to the build agency, while the media agency is judged on the resulting numbers.

We hold both ends. The team buying the traffic is the team that can change the product template, fix the feed, or rebuild the step where people are dropping out — in the same sprint, not the next contract.

## Who this is for

Custom commerce work earns its cost when the catalogue or the operations are genuinely awkward — complex variants, subscriptions, multi-channel inventory, or an ERP that has to stay in sync. For a simple catalogue on a standard platform, configuration beats construction and we will tell you that.
